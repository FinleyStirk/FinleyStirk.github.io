Although it is almost certain that I am biased by my recent reading of Frank Herburt's Dune, I feel like it is impossible for me not to draw some kind of parallels between it's world building and that of mistborn. On a superficial level I immediatly made the connection when the metal Atium (which, in the world of mistborn allows the user to breifly see a short distance into the future) was introduced into the plot and it caused me to instantly think about the Spice of Arrakhis which holds similar properties in the world of Dune. However, I feel like the similarities are far more endemic than this first observation.

The ruling class of nobility follow a very similar feudal system to that of Dune, with different great houses owning land but ultimately being at the mercy of their overlord, that being "The Lord Ruler" in mistborn and the "Padishah Emperor" of Dune. In both societies, the great houses power are kept in balence by the ruler's ability to make artificial scarcity of the precience granting resource.

The next point I make, may be relevant to the stage of the book I am in, as I am sure I will later learn much more about the character for the Lord Ruler who, as of chapter 29 is still steeped in mystery. However, from what is presented so far he bears a remarcable resembalence to the character of Leto Atreidies in God Emporer of Dune. In both they are considered to be Imortal incarnations of the divine who exert an iron grip on man kind through their ability to create artifical scarcity of the precience granting resource. Both characters are deaply contemplative about their role in the prophecies of mankind and see themselves as carrying a form of great burden. They both chronicle their philosophical musings in great detail. In addition both seem to reflect the immense lonliness that comes with great power. They also are both so old and have spent so many millenia shaping the current world that they are the only people who truly know how the world was before they shaped it into their twisted image. Leto and the Lord ruler's subjects both despise and fear them while also barely even understanding the extent of their power. In a sense I think both authors are exploring similar ideas from different viewpoints. The most notable difference is that God Emporrer is mostly explored through the lense of Leto, and we also have the advantage of the previous books of Dune to have a strong grasp of why and how the world came to be the way it is. The perspective of Mistborn comes purely from those living in a world where we as the audience are uncertain of how it came to be the way it is.
