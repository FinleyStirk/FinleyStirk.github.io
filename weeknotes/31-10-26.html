Dissertation Progress

After realising that, even if I deleted every file off my laptop, I still wouldn't be able to store the Tessera embeddings for the UK, I've decided to focus on a smaller area of analysis. The area I've chosen is the New Forest. I first chose this area for its abundance of GBIF data (almost 6 million with position and uncertainty) and the fact it is a national park.

I had a quick scare in my weekly meeting with Michael as he very reasonably asked me if the New Forest would be a problem due to it being a massive forest, and thus lacking in habitat diversity... However luckily for me I went to check this with the habitat map he kindly provided and I saw that there is plenty of diversity in habitat types for this to be a valid area of investigation!

However with a new area of investigation I had to take a step back and do a similar evaluation to the one I did for Madagascar where I analysed the potential different causes for the uncertainty in the GBIF data. Firstly I found that the number of obscured datapoints was much lower (as you would expect compared to Madagascar due to fewer endangered species). In addition I found another source of systematic error in the data that wasn't present in Madagascar. After doing some investigation I plotted the coordinates of some of the most common uncertainty values and was surprised when I saw the following map.

What I was seeing here turned out to be a common practice in a lot of UK datasets such as the UK Butterfly Monitoring Scheme (UKBMS) where records have their position and uncertainty based on OS grid squares. This explained the various spikes at different uncertainty values, as their uncertainty was registered as the diagonal length of the square, which varied depending on what resolution the OS grid was set to.

As you can see the data is absolutely dominated by these grid squares with massive uncertainty, which is something I'm going to have to consider the significance of later, but for now I am not too concerned as even without them there are plenty of datapoints for me to use!
